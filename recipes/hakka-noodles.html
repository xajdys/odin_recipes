Hakka Noodles

Ingredients

    The Base

  • 6 oz (approx. 170g) Hakka noodles (or any thin wheat/eggless noodles)
  • 1 tbsp Oil (for tossing)
  • 6 cups Water (for boiling)
  • 1 tsp Salt

    • The Veggies & Aromatics

  • 2 tbsp Cooking oil (high smoke point oils like peanut or canola work best)
  • 1 tbsp Garlic, finely chopped
  • 1 tsp Ginger, finely chopped
  • 2 Green chilies, slit lengthwise (adjust to taste)
  • ½ cup Spring onion whites, chopped
  • ½ cup Spring onion greens, chopped (for garnish)
  • ½ cup Onion, thinly sliced
  • ½ cup Carrot, cut into thin matchsticks (julienned)
  • ½ cup Capsicum/Bell pepper, thinly sliced
  • ½ cup Cabbage, shredded

    • The Sauces & Seasoning

  • 1 tbsp Soy sauce (dark or regular)
  • 1 tsp Green chili sauce or Red chili sauce
  • 1 tsp White vinegar
  • ¼ tsp Black pepper powder (freshly crushed is best)
  • Salt to taste (go easy, as sauces already contain salt)

Directions

    1. Prep the Noodles (The "Al Dente" Rule)

  1. Bring 6 cups of water to a rolling boil in a large pot with 1 tsp of salt.
  2. Add the noodles and cook according to the package instructions—but subtract 1 minute. You want them al dente (cooked but still firm to the bite), usually about 4–5 minutes.
  3. Drain the noodles immediately into a colander and rinse them thoroughly with cold water to stop the cooking process.
  4. Drizzle 1 tbsp of oil over the noodles and gently toss them. This keeps them from sticking together into a giant clump.

    2. The Stir-Fry (High Heat is Key)

    Pro Tip: Indo-Chinese cooking moves fast. Have all your chopped veggies and sauces right next to the stove before you turn on the heat.

  1. Heat 2 tbsp of oil in a large wok or a wide skillet over high heat until it’s smoking hot.
  2. Add the chopped garlic, ginger, and green chilies. Sauté for just 10–15 seconds until fragrant (don't let the garlic burn).
  3. Add the sliced onions and spring onion whites. Stir-fry for 1 minute.
  4. Toss in the carrots, cabbage, and bell peppers. Stir-fry on high heat for 2 minutes. The goal is to cook them slightly while retaining their crunch and vibrant color.

    3. Bring It All Together

  1. Lower the heat slightly and add the cooked noodles into the wok.
  2. Drizzle the soy sauce, chili sauce, and vinegar evenly over the noodles.
  3. Sprinkle the black pepper powder and a pinch of salt.
  4. Turn the heat back up to high. Using two spatulas (or tongs), gently toss and stir-fry the noodles for 1–2 minutes until the sauces are well incorporated and the noodles are heated through. Be gentle so you don't break the strands!
  5. Turn off the heat and garnish generously with the spring onion greens.
